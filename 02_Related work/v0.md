While many tools exist that deal with analysis of large document sets, to the best of our knowledge no integrated tool with the dedicated purpose of creating process models from document sets exists. Therefore, here we explore related tools that partially address this problem.

In total, eleven AI-assist modeling tools are explored during the literature review \ref{tab:lr}. 

Among the 12 hits of the first search query (“allintitle: process modeling generative AI”), the tool ProMoAI \cite{kourani2024promoai} was identified. It is the first shown tool in the academic area leveraging LLM for automated process model generation introduced in 2024 and is frequently referenced in subsequent work. ProMoAI enables the transformation of natural language into process models and supports iterative refinement through feedback loops, demonstrating the feasibility of LLM-based process modeling.

In the second keyword search _“allintitle: process modeling LLM”_, among 16 hits, 5 tools are identified. The first noticable two are **BPMN-Chatbot** \cite{kopke2024introducing, kopke2024efficient} and **BPMN Assistant** \cite{licardo2026bpmn}. BPMN-Chatbot is in 2024, focuses on token efficiency and cost reduction while maintaining high model correctness, achieving up to 94% fewer tokens compared to alternatives  . BPMN Assistant \cite{licardo2026bpmn} represents a more recent development, emphasizing structured intermediate representations (e.g., JSON) to improve reliability, editing capabilities, and performance in interactive modeling scenarios  .

Another approach, **KICoPro**, is introduced in \cite{lauer2026human}, focusing on human-in-the-loop process modeling, where users collaborate with AI systems during model creation.

Furthermore, **HyperMod** is introduced in \cite{pardo2025direct}, which combines LLM-based generation with a direct manipulation interface, allowing users to refine process models interactively by selecting and modifying elements within the diagram  .


The second query (“allintitle: process modeling LLM”) yielded several additional tools. Notably, BPMN-Chatbot \cite{kopke2024introducing, kopke2024efficient} introduces a token-efficient conversational approach for process modeling, while BPMN Assist \cite{licardo2026bpmn} represents a more recent advancement in this area. Further contributions include KICoPro \cite{lauer2026human}, which incorporates human-in-the-loop mechanisms, and HyperMode \cite{pardo2025direct}, which focuses on direct model generation. Additionally, BPMNGen, initially proposed in \cite{horner2025towards} and later extended in \cite{horner2026automatically}, demonstrates iterative improvements in automated BPMN generation.

  

A broader search combining “process modeling” and “large language” revealed the tool PRODIGY \cite{ziche2024llm4pm}. Similarly, the query “allintitle: conversational process modeling” identified AutoBPMN.AI \cite{klievtsova2025autobpmn} as the only dedicated conversational modeling approach.

  

Finally, combining “BPMN” with “generative AI” and related terms led to the identification of additional tools, including BPMN-Chatbot++ \cite{safan2025bpmn}, LLM4BPMNGen \cite{costa2025llm4bpmngen}, and Nala2BPMN \cite{nour2024nala2bpmn}.

  

In summary, although a growing number of tools leverage LLMs to support process modeling, none provide a fully integrated solution for automatically deriving complete process models from large document collections.


Finally, the tool **BPMNGen** is first introduced in \cite{horner2025towards} and later further developed and evaluated in \cite{horner2026automatically}. BPMNGen provides a conversational framework that enables users to iteratively generate and refine BPMN models from natural language descriptions, with empirical evaluations showing strong semantic quality for simple to moderately complex processes  .

  

In the search attempt combining _“process modeling”_ and _“large language”_, one additional tool, **PRODIGY**, is identified in \cite{ziche2024llm4pm}, which emphasizes conversational interaction and iterative model refinement.

  

In the search query _“allintitle: conversational process modeling”_, the tool **AutoBPMN.AI** \cite{klievtsova2025autobpmn} is found, highlighting the trend toward conversational interfaces in BPM.

  

In a further search combining _“BPMN”_ with _“generative AI”_, _“LLM(s)”_, or _“large language”_, three more tools are identified. These include **BPMN-Chatbot++** \cite{safan2025bpmn}, which extends earlier chatbot approaches, as well as a framework-oriented contribution in \cite{safan2025framework}. Additionally, **LLM4BPMNGen** \cite{costa2025llm4bpmngen} focuses on automated BPMN generation pipelines, and **Nala2BPMN** \cite{nour2024nala2bpmn} proposes a modular architecture for extracting and structuring process elements from textual descriptions.

  

In summary, while numerous tools leverage LLMs for generating process models from natural language, they predominantly focus on single textual inputs and interactive refinement. There is still no integrated solution specifically designed to process and synthesize large document sets into coherent, unified process models. This highlights a clear research gap and motivates the need for approaches that bridge document analysis and process model generation in a scalable and integrated manner.
