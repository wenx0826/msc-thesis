## 6.2 Interaction and Usability

> Participants interacted with the system through a combination of document reading, text selection, and process modelling. Overall, the interaction was workable in practice, allowing users to complete modelling tasks within the system. However, several issues emerged during use, particularly in document interaction and specific system features.

> With regard to document interaction, participants showed a clear preference for using external PDF viewers when reading documents. This suggests that the document interaction capabilities of the system do not yet match the usability of dedicated tools, especially in terms of readability and navigation within longer documents.

> In addition, several feature-level issues were identified. For example, some participants questioned the necessity of certain interaction steps, such as the review step, indicating that these elements may not align well with users’ expectations or preferred workflows. Other aspects of the system required additional effort to understand, particularly when users encountered unfamiliar interaction patterns.

> Some of these difficulties appear to be related to the learnability of the system. As the workflow introduces new ways of interacting with documents and process models, users may require an initial familiarisation phase before becoming fully comfortable with the system.

> Overall, while the interaction was sufficient to support task completion, these findings suggest that improvements in document handling, feature design, and learnability would enhance the overall user experience.


> Participants interacted with the system through document reading, text selection, and process modelling. While the interaction supported task completion, several challenges were identified during use.

> Some issues were related to specific interaction design choices. For example, the review step following model generation was perceived by some participants as unnecessary, as the generated models were already of sufficient quality. Participants expressed a preference for more streamlined interaction, suggesting that such steps could be made optional through configurable settings.


> In addition, certain aspects of the interaction required users to become familiar with the system’s logic. These challenges appear to be related to the learnability of the system, as the workflow introduces new interaction patterns. While this required an initial familiarisation phase, it was not perceived as a major barrier to use.

> A more prominent issue was identified in document interaction. Participants showed a preference for external PDF viewers, indicating limitations in the system’s document display capabilities. Although this does not prevent users from completing modelling tasks, it affects the overall interaction experience, particularly as document reading represents the starting point of the workflow.


> Overall, these findings suggest that while the interaction is sufficient to support modelling tasks, improvements in interaction design, learnability, and document handling would enhance usability.


## Conceptual Understanding of AI-Assisted Process Modelling
> Beyond interaction-level challenges, participants encountered difficulties in developing a conceptual understanding of AI-assisted process modelling. In particular, challenges arose in understanding how the system generates and modifies process models based on user input.

>   

> While the initial generation of models from selected text was generally well understood, subsequent AI-assisted modifications were less clear to participants. In these cases, the underlying logic of the system—especially how inputs such as selected text and the current model were combined—was not sufficiently transparent.

>   

> As a result, participants were often unable to anticipate how their actions would influence the generated outputs, which occasionally led to mismatches between expected and actual results. This suggests that users were attempting to form a mental model of the system’s AI-assisted behaviour, but the system did not adequately support this process.

>   

> In addition, some mismatches appeared to be related to misaligned expectations regarding the capabilities of AI-assisted modelling. Participants occasionally expected the system to perform more complex or implicitly specified transformations than it could support, indicating a gap between user expectations and system behaviour.

>   

> These findings highlight the importance of supporting users’ conceptual understanding of AI-assisted modelling, not only through improved transparency of system logic, but also through clearer communication of system capabilities and limitations.

## User Expectations and Advanced Capabilities


> In addition to the challenges identified during interaction, participants also expressed expectations for more advanced system capabilities. In particular, there was a clear interest in more intelligent support for working with documents, such as the ability to search for relevant information or automatically identify process-related content.

>   

> These expectations suggest that users do not only rely on manual interaction with documents, but also anticipate more proactive assistance from the system in understanding and extracting relevant information. This points towards the potential value of incorporating document intelligence capabilities to support document-based process modelling.

>   

> Furthermore, these expectations align with the observed challenges in conceptual understanding, indicating that users not only require clearer explanations of system behaviour, but also expect the system to perform more advanced modelling tasks. Together, these findings highlight opportunities for extending the system beyond basic interaction towards more intelligent and adaptive support.


> Overall, the findings suggest that while the system is capable of supporting document-based process modelling in an integrated workflow, important challenges remain at both the interaction and conceptual levels. In particular, the results indicate that effective use of AI-assisted modelling systems depends not only on functional integration and usability, but also on users’ ability to understand and anticipate system behaviour.

>   

> This highlights the importance of designing systems that better support human–AI collaboration, through improved transparency, clearer communication of system capabilities, and more intelligent assistance in working with documents. Addressing these aspects is essential for enabling more effective and reliable use of AI in document-based process modelling.



> Taken together, these findings suggest that while the system is capable of supporting document-based process modelling within an integrated workflow, effective use of AI-assisted modelling extends beyond functional support. The results highlight that both interaction quality and users’ conceptual understanding of AI behaviour play a critical role in shaping the overall modelling experience. Addressing these aspects—through improved interaction design, greater transparency, and more advanced document intelligence—will be essential for enabling more effective and reliable human–AI collaboration in document-based process modelling.
