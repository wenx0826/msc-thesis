## X.1 Usability and User Experience in Document-Based Process Modeling (RQ2)

The evaluation results suggest that designing usable document-based process modeling systems requires careful consideration of both interaction design and user understanding of system behavior. While the proposed system was perceived as usable after a period of familiarization, participants encountered challenges in understanding how to effectively interact with the system and how different components are connected within the overall workflow.
  

One key observation is that usability in such systems extends beyond traditional interface design. Although basic interactions were considered intuitive, more advanced functionalities—such as model refinement, version handling, and text-based interaction—required additional effort to understand. This indicates that learnability remains an important factor, particularly in systems that combine multiple interaction paradigms, including document interaction, model manipulation, and AI-based generation.

  

Beyond interaction challenges, the findings highlight the importance of supporting user understanding of system behavior. Participants reported difficulties in understanding how their inputs—such as selected text and prompts—were processed and how AI contributes to the generated models. This suggests that usability in AI-supported systems is closely tied to the user’s ability to form an accurate mental model of how the system operates.

  

In particular, the results indicate that users require clarity at multiple levels. At the input level, users need to understand which parts of the provided text are considered during model generation. At the task level, they need to understand how different operations, such as refinement and regeneration, influence the outcome. Without such clarity, users may struggle to interpret results and to effectively guide the modeling process.

  

Another important aspect concerns the role of document interaction. Unlike traditional systems where documents primarily serve as background information, the proposed system integrates document reading, interpretation, and modeling within a single environment. As a result, the quality of document rendering and interaction becomes an important factor in the overall user experience, as it directly affects how users understand and engage with the source material.

  

Overall, these findings suggest that designing effective document-based process modeling tools requires not only intuitive interaction mechanisms but also explicit support for understanding system behavior, input usage, and the role of AI within the workflow.

## X.2 Implications for the Design of AI-Supported Modeling Systems

Based on the identified challenges, several design implications can be derived for future systems in this domain.

First, systems should provide clearer feedback on how user inputs are used during model generation. Making the influence of selected text and prompts more explicit can help users better understand and control the modeling process. This includes indicating which parts of the input are considered and how they contribute to specific model elements.

  

Second, AI-supported systems should offer mechanisms that allow users to clarify system behavior through interaction. The lack of interactive feedback in the current system made it difficult for users to resolve uncertainties. Incorporating more interactive elements—such as the ability to query or explore model decisions—could support a deeper understanding of the system.

  

Third, systems should support the interpretation and evaluation of generated models. Users expressed the need for better explanations of modeling decisions, identification of ambiguities, and comparison between different model versions. Providing such support can help users validate and refine generated models more effectively.

  

Fourth, document interaction should be treated as an integral part of the system design. Since users rely on the system to read and interpret documents, high-quality document rendering and navigation are important for supporting the overall workflow.

  

Finally, the integration of AI functionalities should be carefully designed to align with user expectations. Rather than treating AI as a separate component, embedding AI capabilities within the user’s workflow—particularly within document interaction—may lead to a more coherent and effective user experience.

## X.3 Impact on Modeling Effectiveness and Comparison to Traditional Approaches (RQ3)


The evaluation results indicate that the proposed system has the potential to enhance certain aspects of process modeling, particularly in the early stages of model creation. The ability to generate initial process model drafts allows users to quickly obtain a starting point, which can reduce the effort required to initiate the modeling process compared to traditional manual approaches.
  

However, the findings also suggest that this potential is currently limited by challenges related to usability and system understanding. In particular, the lack of clarity regarding how models are generated and how inputs are processed can reduce user confidence in the results. As a result, users may be hesitant to rely on the system for more complex or critical modeling tasks.
  

Compared to traditional modeling workflows, where users maintain full control and understanding of the modeling process, the introduction of AI shifts part of the control to the system. While this can increase efficiency, it also introduces uncertainty. The results suggest that this trade-off must be carefully managed, as increased automation does not necessarily lead to improved effectiveness if users do not trust or understand the system.
  

Overall, the proposed system can enhance modeling effectiveness in terms of speed and support for initial model creation, but its impact on overall effectiveness depends on improving transparency, usability, and user confidence. Achieving this balance is essential for enabling effective human-in-the-loop workflows in AI-supported modeling systems.
