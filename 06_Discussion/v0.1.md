## Design Implications for Document-Based Process Modelling (RQ2)


This section addresses RQ2 by reflecting on how the design of the proposed system supports usable and effective process modelling within an integrated workflow. In particular, it focuses on the role of integration, usability, learnability, and transparency in shaping the overall user experience.

  

A central design contribution of the system lies in the integration of document interaction and process modelling within a unified workflow. By enabling users to directly select relevant text and generate corresponding process models, the system reduces the need to switch between separate tools and supports a more coherent modelling process. This integration was generally perceived as intuitive and beneficial, particularly in scenarios involving multiple documents and iterative model development.

  

From a usability perspective, the overall workflow was considered clear and easy to follow. However, some interaction mechanisms were initially perceived as unintuitive, requiring users to familiarise themselves with the system’s interaction logic. These findings suggest that certain usability challenges are related to learnability rather than fundamental design flaws. In this regard, the system reflects a trade-off between immediate intuitiveness and the ability to support more advanced interactions, indicating that users may adapt to the system over time.

  

A more critical design challenge concerns the lack of transparency in AI-assisted model generation and modification. Participants reported uncertainty regarding how inputs—such as selected text and existing models—are incorporated into the generation process, making it difficult to anticipate or interpret system outputs. This highlights the importance of transparency in the design of AI-assisted modelling systems, as users need to understand input–output relationships in order to maintain trust and effectively guide the modelling process.

  

In addition, the quality of document rendering was identified as an important factor influencing usability. Since the system relies heavily on interaction with source documents, limitations in document display—particularly for PDF files—can negatively affect the overall user experience. This suggests that effective document-based modelling depends not only on modelling functionalities, but also on the quality and reliability of document interaction.

  

Overall, the findings indicate that integrating functionalities within a unified workflow provides a strong foundation for document-based process modelling. At the same time, the results highlight key design considerations, including the need to balance usability and learnability, ensure transparency in AI-assisted functionalities, and support high-quality interaction with source documents.

## Perceived Benefits and Limitations (RQ3)

  

This section addresses RQ3 by examining the perceived benefits and limitations of the proposed system in supporting document-based process modelling from the users’ perspective.

  

Participants identified several benefits of the system, particularly in relation to its integrated workflow and model management capabilities. The ability to directly link selected text segments to corresponding model elements was consistently perceived as valuable, as it facilitates traceability between source documents and process models. In addition, the versioning functionality was highlighted as one of the most beneficial features, as it supports iterative model development and enables users to track and revisit changes over time. Furthermore, the integration of document interaction and process modelling within a single environment was considered advantageous, especially when working with multiple documents or more complex modelling scenarios. These aspects collectively contribute to the perceived usefulness of the system in supporting document-based modelling tasks.

  

Despite these advantages, several limitations were also identified. A key limitation concerns the lack of transparency in AI-assisted model generation and modification. Participants reported uncertainty regarding how inputs are processed and how outputs are produced, which can reduce their confidence in the system and make it more difficult to interpret or control the generated models. In addition, certain interaction mechanisms were initially perceived as unintuitive, requiring users to understand specific interaction logic before using the system effectively. While these challenges may be mitigated through experience, they nevertheless impact the initial usability of the system.

  

Another limitation relates to the quality of document rendering, particularly for PDF documents. Since the system relies heavily on interaction with source materials, suboptimal document display negatively affects the overall user experience. Although this issue does not directly concern the modelling functionality itself, it influences how effectively users can work with the system in practice.

  

Overall, the findings suggest that the system is particularly beneficial in complex modelling scenarios involving multiple documents and iterative refinement, where its integrated workflow and model management features provide clear advantages. In simpler use cases, however, these benefits are less pronounced, indicating that the added value of the system depends on the complexity of the modelling task.

## Implications for System Design

  

The findings of this study provide several implications for the design of document-based process modelling systems. First, the integration of document interaction and process modelling within a unified workflow appears to be a key factor in supporting effective modelling, particularly in complex scenarios.

  

Second, the results highlight the importance of balancing usability and learnability. While fully intuitive interactions are desirable, more advanced functionalities may require users to learn and adapt to specific interaction patterns. Designing systems that support this learning process without overwhelming users remains an important challenge.

  

Third, transparency emerges as a critical requirement for AI-assisted modelling systems. Users need to understand how inputs are transformed into outputs in order to build trust and effectively collaborate with automated functionalities. This suggests that future systems should prioritise mechanisms for making model generation processes more interpretable and controllable.

  

Finally, the quality of document interaction plays a crucial role in overall usability. Since modelling activities are closely tied to source documents, ensuring high-quality document rendering and interaction is essential for supporting efficient and effective modelling workflows.

## Limitations of the Study

  

This study has several limitations. First, the evaluation was conducted with a relatively small number of expert users, which may limit the generalisability of the findings. Second, the qualitative nature of the evaluation focuses on user perceptions rather than objective performance measures, meaning that improvements in efficiency or modelling accuracy were not quantitatively assessed.

  

In addition, the evaluation scenarios were limited in scope and may not fully capture the diversity of real-world modelling contexts. Finally, some advanced functionalities suggested by participants, particularly in relation to AI-assisted modelling, were beyond the scope of the current system and therefore could not be evaluated.

---

## Future Work


Future work can build upon the findings of this study in several directions. A key area for improvement is enhancing the transparency of AI-assisted model generation, for example by providing clearer representations of how inputs are processed and how outputs are derived.

  

Further research could also explore more advanced AI capabilities, such as context-aware interactions, improved integration of document understanding techniques, and more sophisticated support for model refinement. In particular, participants expressed a need for more task-level support, indicating the importance of guiding users in structuring and interpreting modelling tasks from documents.

  

In addition, improving the quality of document rendering, especially for PDF documents, is essential for enhancing usability in document-based modelling scenarios. Finally, future evaluations could include larger and more diverse user groups, as well as quantitative studies to assess the system’s impact on modelling performance and efficiency.
