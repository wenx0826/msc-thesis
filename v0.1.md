# **7 Conclusion**

  

This thesis addressed the challenge of supporting process modeling from unstructured documents by proposing and developing an integrated, document-based process modeling tool. The work aimed to improve the efficiency and usability of transforming textual information into structured process models by leveraging large language models (LLMs) in a human-in-the-loop setting.

  

The research was guided by three research questions.

  

**RQ1: How do existing approaches and tools support process modeling from documents?**

The analysis of related work revealed that existing approaches, including LLM-based process modeling tools and document interaction systems, provide valuable capabilities such as natural language-based model generation and conversational interaction. However, these tools often operate in separated environments, lacking tight integration between document handling and process modeling. As a result, users are required to manually extract and transfer relevant information, which introduces inefficiencies and increases cognitive load. This work concludes that current approaches provide partial support but do not fully address the need for a seamless, document-centered modeling workflow.

  

**RQ2: How can an integrated system be designed to support process modeling from documents?**

To address the identified limitations, this thesis proposed and implemented an integrated system that combines document management, text interaction, and process modeling within a unified interface. The system enables users to directly select text passages from documents as input for LLM-based model generation, establishes traceability between source text and generated models, and supports the management of multiple models and their relationships. The design demonstrates that integrating these functionalities into a single workflow can significantly improve the efficiency and coherence of the modeling process. At the same time, the results highlight the importance of careful interaction design, particularly regarding transparency and user control in AI-assisted features.

  

**RQ3: How do users perceive the proposed system?**

The evaluation with domain experts showed that the system is generally perceived as usable, intuitive, and effective in supporting document-based process modeling tasks. Participants particularly appreciated the integrated workflow, the clear linkage between text and models, and the ability to manage complex modeling scenarios involving multiple documents. However, several limitations were identified, including challenges in understanding the system’s internal logic, especially in AI-assisted modifications, and minor usability issues related to interaction and document rendering. These findings indicate that while the system provides clear benefits, further improvements are necessary to enhance transparency, usability, and overall user experience.

  

Overall, this work demonstrates that an integrated, document-based approach to process modeling is both feasible and beneficial. By combining LLM capabilities with user interaction in a unified environment, the proposed system contributes a novel and practical solution to the field of process modeling.

  

Despite these contributions, the work has several limitations. The evaluation was conducted with a small number of participants and focused on qualitative feedback, which limits the generalizability of the findings. In addition, certain technical aspects, such as document rendering and advanced AI-assisted features, remain in an early stage of development.

  

Future work can address these limitations by conducting larger-scale evaluations, improving document visualization and interaction mechanisms, and enhancing AI capabilities. In particular, increasing the transparency and explainability of LLM-based model generation, as well as incorporating retrieval-augmented approaches and model quality assessment features, represent promising directions for further research.

  

In conclusion, this thesis provides both conceptual and practical contributions to document-based process modeling and highlights the potential of integrated, human-centered AI systems to support complex modeling tasks more effectively.

#### 7.1 Answers to Research Questions

This study aimed to address the challenges of converting unstructured documents into structured process models by leveraging Large Language Models (LLMs) within an integrated environment. The following sections summarize the answers to the research questions based on the conducted literature review, system design, implementation, and evaluation.

**Answer to RQ1: How do existing approaches and tools support process modeling from documents?**

The review of existing literature and tools (such as ProMoAI, AutoBPMN.AI, and NotebookLM) revealed that while LLMs have shown great potential in automating process modeling, current approaches suffer from a lack of integration. Most existing tools operate in a "separate interface" paradigm, where the document and the modeling canvas are distinct entities. Users are required to manually extract information from documents and input it into a chatbot or modeling interface. This separation makes it difficult to verify the accuracy of the generated models against the source text and creates friction in the modeling workflow. Furthermore, existing tools often lack robust mechanisms for maintaining bidirectional traceability between the source text fragments and the generated model elements.

**Answer to RQ2: How can an integrated system be designed to support process modeling from documents?**

To address the limitations identified in RQ1, this thesis designed and proposed the **Document-Based Process Modeler (DBPM)**. The system adopts a "Read-Select-Generate" workflow integrated within a **3-Pane Layout** (Document View, Model Canvas, Properties/Control). This design allows users to directly select text passages from unstructured documents (PDF/DOCX) and trigger LLM-based model generation with context awareness. A core design feature is the implementation of **bidirectional traceability**: the system establishes explicit visual links between the selected text fragments (using W3C Web Annotation standards) and the generated BPMN-like model nodes. Furthermore, the architecture supports a Human-in-the-loop mechanism, allowing for iterative refinement (Regeneration, Modification) of the model based on the original document context.

**Answer to RQ3: How do users perceive the proposed system?**

The proposed DBPM tool was evaluated with four domain experts through task-based scenarios and semi-structured interviews. The results indicate that the system is perceived as **usable, intuitive, and effective** for managing complex document-based modeling tasks. Experts particularly valued the **versioning functionality** and the ability to manage multiple documents and models within a unified interface. The evaluation confirmed that the integrated workflow significantly reduces the friction of switching between tools. However, users also provided critical feedback regarding the need for improved **transparency in the LLM's regeneration logic** and better **PDF rendering capabilities**. Despite these specific usability issues, the experts expressed a positive intention to use the tool for future tasks involving the management of multiple interrelated documents and models.

#### 7.2 Summary of Contributions

This work contributes to both research and practice in the field of Information Systems and Business Process Management:

1. **Artifact Contribution:** A novel web-based tool (DBPM) that bridges the gap between unstructured documents and structured process models.
2. **Design Knowledge:** The design principles of integrating a "Document-Model" symbiosis through offset-based anchoring and a 3-Pane interaction paradigm.
3. **Empirical Evidence:** Insights from expert evaluations regarding the usability of hybrid Human-LLM modeling workflows and the importance of traceability in AI-assisted modeling.

#### 7.3 Limitations and Future Work

Despite the positive evaluation, this study has limitations. The current prototype relies on external LLM APIs (AutoBPMN.AI), which limits control over the generation logic. Furthermore, the PDF text extraction and rendering capabilities are still basic.

Future work should focus on:

- **Enhancing AI Transparency:** Implementing Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) to provide fine-grained traceability, showing exactly which sentences were used to generate specific model nodes.
- **Improving Document Handling:** Optimizing the rendering of complex PDF layouts to enhance readability.
- **Advanced Interaction:** Adding conversational interfaces (Chatbots) directly on the canvas to allow users to ask questions about the model or request modifications through natural language.
