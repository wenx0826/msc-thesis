> **The proposed system is evaluated through a qualitative study with expert users, focusing on usability, perceived usefulness, and user feedback.**

> To assess the quality and relevance of the proposed system, this research adopts a qualitative evaluation approach that is closely aligned with the research questions. Rather than focusing on objective performance metrics, the evaluation emphasises how the system is experienced and perceived by its intended users in the context of document-based process modelling. This is particularly important given that the contribution of this work lies not only in functional capabilities, but also in the integration of document interaction and process modelling within a coherent workflow.



> In relation to RQ2, which addresses the design of a document-based process modelling system, the evaluation examines the system’s usability and its ability to effectively support modelling tasks. This includes how intuitively users can interact with the system, how clearly the workflow is structured, and how well the integration of functionalities—such as document interaction, model generation, and model management—supports the modelling process. The evaluation therefore provides insights into whether the proposed design successfully enables a usable and effective modelling experience within an integrated environment.


> With respect to RQ3, the evaluation focuses on identifying the perceived benefits and limitations of the system from the users’ perspective. Rather than attempting to measure objective improvements in efficiency or performance, the study investigates how users assess the usefulness of the system in practice, which aspects are considered beneficial, and which challenges or limitations arise during use. This perspective is essential for understanding the practical value of the system, particularly in complex modelling scenarios where interaction with documents and models is closely intertwined.

>   

> To achieve this, a qualitative user study with expert participants is conducted, combining task-based interaction with semi-structured interviews. Participants are asked to engage with the system through representative modelling tasks and to reflect on their experience, including what they found intuitive, useful, or problematic. This approach allows for the collection of rich, experience-based insights into both the strengths and weaknesses of the system.

>   

> Overall, the evaluation is designed to provide a comprehensive understanding of how the proposed system performs in supporting document-based process modelling, not in terms of purely quantitative improvements, but in terms of usability, perceived usefulness, and the balance between benefits and limitations. In doing so, it directly addresses the research questions and provides the basis for discussing the effectiveness of the design and its implications for future development.
