## Procedure

In this section, I will present the evaluation of the proposed system.

The evaluation was conducted as a qualitative user study with four process modeling experts. The goal was to assess the system’s usability, usefulness, and potential for real-world application.

Each session lasted around 40 minutes and consisted of three parts. First, a short introduction and demonstration of the system were provided. Then, participants completed two tasks.

The first task was based on a fictional B2B order-processing scenario using two DOCX documents, allowing participants to explore the system in a controlled setting. The second task involved a real-world SOP document in PDF format, representing a more realistic and complex scenario.

Participants were free to interact with the system without strict instructions, in order to observe natural usage behavior.

After completing the tasks, participants answered open-ended questions regarding positive aspects, limitations, desired improvements, and their willingness to use the tool in the future.

## Overall Perception
Now I will present the evaluation results.
Overall, participants perceived the system as intuitive and usable, and they were able to complete the modeling tasks successfully.

## Positive Aspect
Several positive aspects were highlighted.


### Integrate LLM Service: Fast and Good quality
First, the integrated LLM service was mentioned by the participants fast and good effort.

Second, the process–subprocess relationship feature was perceived as convenient, especially when dealing with complex systems.  
Third, model versioning was frequently mentioned as helpful for managing iterative changes.

In addition, participants appreciated the integration of contextual information, such as selected text and prompt history, into the model data.

Finally, regarding visualization, the project graph was considered helpful, but its representation of node types and versions was sometimes unclear and could be improved.

## Problems in Interaction and Use
However, some limitations were also identified.


### Modeling Process: Unintuitive to start a new modeling
For the modeling process, some interactions were not intuitive, such as the need to explicitly deselect a model to start a new one. Also, the initial review step was sometimes considered unnecessary.

### Modeling Process: Unnecessary review step (of the initial generated model)

Another important issue is the lack of transparency in the modeling logic. Participants reported that they did not fully understand how the system processes inputs or modifies models during interaction.
