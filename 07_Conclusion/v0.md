 In this paper, we presented a system that supports the generation of process models from textual documents using large language models. The system enables users to create and iteratively refine process models while maintaining traceability between textual inputs and generated models.

The qualitative evaluation with domain experts indicates that the system is intuitive to use and effective in generating initial process models, particularly for early-stage exploration. Features such as versioning and the explicit linkage between text and models were perceived as especially valuable. At the same time, the results highlight several challenges, including the need for improved interaction clarity, better transparency of system behavior, and stronger support for document understanding.

Overall, the findings suggest that LLM-based approaches have significant potential for supporting document-driven process modelling. However, further improvements are required to enhance usability, reliability, and user trust.

Future work includes improving document-grounded modelling, for example through retrieval-based techniques, enhancing explainability of model generation, and refining the user interface to better support iterative modelling workflows.

This work presented and evaluated a system for document-based process modelling that integrates text interaction, AI-assisted model generation, and model management. The qualitative evaluation indicates that the system is generally perceived as usable, intuitive, and effective in supporting modelling tasks within a unified environment.

Key strengths include the seamless workflow from text to model, clear text–model linkage, and strong support for managing model relationships. In particular, the versioning functionality was identified as highly valuable for iterative modelling. At the same time, challenges were identified, especially regarding the limited transparency of AI-assisted modifications, which affected users’ understanding and trust. Additional usability issues and limitations in document handling further highlight areas for improvement.

Overall, the system shows strong potential for complex, document-based modelling scenarios. Future work should focus on improving AI transparency, interaction design, and document integration, as well as incorporating more advanced AI-supported features.

This thesis addressed the challenges of document-based process modelling by proposing a system that integrates document interaction and process modelling within a unified workflow. The goal was to support users in deriving process models directly from textual sources while maintaining traceability and improving modelling efficiency.

The findings demonstrate that integrating document interaction and process modelling can effectively support modelling tasks, particularly in more complex scenarios involving multiple documents and iterative refinement. The system’s design highlights the importance of explicit text–model linkage, versioning support, and a coherent workflow. At the same time, the results reveal key design challenges, including the need to balance usability and learnability, ensure transparency in AI-assisted model generation, and support high-quality interaction with source documents.

From the users’ perspective, the system provides clear benefits in terms of integration, traceability, and model management. However, several limitations were identified, particularly regarding the lack of transparency in AI-assisted functionalities, certain unintuitive interaction mechanisms, and limitations in document rendering. These findings suggest that the effectiveness of such systems depends not only on modelling capabilities but also on the quality of interaction design and user understanding of system behaviour.

Overall, this work contributes to the understanding of how document-based process modelling systems can be designed to support both usability and effectiveness. Future work should focus on improving transparency, enhancing interaction flexibility, and further integrating advanced AI capabilities to better support user needs in complex modelling scenarios.
