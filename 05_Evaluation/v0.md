## Evaluation Method

A qualitative evaluation was conducted with four expert users to gain insights into the usability and potential of the proposed system. The evaluation was carried out in the form of semi-structured interviews.

At the beginning of the evaluation, participants received a brief introduction to the system to familiarise themselves with its main functionalities. The tasks included a simple example designed for participants to explore the system, followed by a more realistic process modelling task based on a real-world document. If time permitted, they could also use the system with their own documents.

After completing the tasks, participants were asked open-ended questions about what they liked and disliked about the system, as well as potential improvements and additional features. Additionally, they were asked whether they would consider using the system in the future to model processes from documents and to explain their reasoning. The interviews lasted approximately 40 minutes on average.

## Evaluation Results

### Overall Assessment and Positive Aspects

Overall, participants perceived the system as usable and intuitive, and capable of supporting document-based process modelling tasks within a unified environment. The basic workflow of selecting relevant text, generating process models, and the linkage between the selected text and the corresponding models was generally perceived as clear and easy to follow. In addition, establishing relationships between models and its subprocesses was perceived as simple and useful. 

Besides the core functionalities of the system, several additional positive aspects were reported by participants. First, the versioning functionality was highlighted as the most valuable feature, as it effectively supports iterative process model development by enabling users to track changes and revisit previous model versions. Second, relevant information—that is, selected text segments and prompt history—was directly embedded within the model XML data; this was considered a well-integrated design feature. Third, logging and statistical information elicited interest among some expert users due to its potential for further analysis. Finally, the network-style visualisation of relationships across models and documents was mentioned by some participants as helpful, especially in the context of larger systems.

### Challenges in Use and Interaction

Despite the generally positive perception of the system, participants also reported several challenges in use and interaction. 

A primary challenge concerned interactions involving AI-assisted model modifications. While the initial generation of models from text was generally well understood, the subsequent AI-assisted modifications were not always immediately intuitive. In these situations, the system’s input logic lacked transparency, making it unclear to participants whether and how the current model and the selected or modified text were included in the generation process. This made it difficult for participants to understand how specific actions influenced the generated outputs and could lead to mismatches between expected and actual outcomes, as the system’s input composition did not always align with participants’ expectations.

In addition to these conceptual challenges, minor usability issues were identified in the interaction design. In particular, the requirement to explicitly deselect the current model before initiating a new modelling step was not immediately intuitive and required users to understand and familiarise themselves with the system’s interaction logic. In addition, the review step following the initial generation of models was perceived by some participants as unnecessary, as the generated models were already of sufficiently high quality.

Finally, limitations in document rendering were reported as a practical constraint. In particular, the display of PDF documents was perceived as suboptimal, which negatively affected the user experience when working with source materials. Although this issue does not directly impact the core modelling functionality, it influences the overall usability of the system in document-based modelling scenarios. 

### Suggested Improvements and Desired Features

Beyond the challenges identified in system use and interaction, a number of improvements and desired features for the system were suggested. These can be grouped into two categories: system-level features and more advanced AI-assisted modelling capabilities.

At the system level, improvements and desired features were suggested across several aspects. The following highlights some of the key suggestions. Firstly, improvements to the visualisation were mentioned, including clearer differentiation and identification of elements (e.g. which type of element it is and which version a node represents), as well as more expressive visual representations (e.g. using size, distance, or other visual properties to convey additional information). Secondly, model comparison was suggested as a valuable feature to help users understand and verify changes between different model versions, making it easier to identify modifications and track model iterations over time. Finally, an AI-assisted document interaction feature was suggested, where relevant information can be retrieved from documents, automatically selected, and directly used as input for model generation, thereby reducing manual effort and time.

The second category concerns advanced AI-assisted modelling capabilities. In particular, participants emphasised the need for fine-grained traceability between source text and model elements, highlighting the importance of establishing explicit and granular links between textual input and corresponding model components. This reflects a strong demand for more precise and grounded model generation in document-based modelling scenarios. Beyond this, participants also suggested more general AI capabilities, including enhanced explainability of model generation, conversational interaction with the system, and support for process model quality evaluation and improvement. While these capabilities are not specific to document-based modelling, they indicate a broader interest in more intelligent and interactive modelling support.


### Future Use Intention

Participants’ responses suggest an intention to use the system in scenarios involving the management and exploration of multiple models and their associated documents. In such cases, features such as clear text-model-linkage, process-subprocess-relationship, and model management functionalities were considered particularly useful. In contrast, for simpler use cases, such as generating a process model from a single document, the system remains a viable option but does not provide a strong advantage over existing approaches. In both cases, participants pointed to the need for more advanced functionalities, particularly in relation to AI-assisted modelling features and improved interaction with documents.

Based on participant feedback, several improvements and additional features were identified. A recurring theme concerns **enhancing transparency and guidance** in the interaction with the system. Participants expressed the need for clearer feedback on how models are generated, for example by indicating which parts of the selected text were actually used, or by highlighting ambiguities in the input that influenced the model. In addition, more explicit explanations of changes between model versions—such as natural language summaries of differences—were suggested to better support iterative refinement.

Another important area relates to **interaction design and usability improvements**. Several participants proposed more intuitive naming of interface elements (e.g., clearer labels for “deselect” or “delete selection”), reducing unnecessary steps such as manual saving, and improving navigation features (e.g., opening projects in new tabs or maintaining scroll positions). Enhancements to document handling, particularly better rendering of PDF files, were also highlighted.

Participants also emphasized the potential of **advanced AI-supported functionalities**. Suggested features include enabling the system to search relevant parts of documents automatically (e.g., retrieval-based approaches), incorporating previously generated models into the context to avoid redundancy, and allowing users to query the system about generated models (e.g., asking for design rationales). Additionally, ideas such as automatic quality assessment of process models, detection of modelling issues (e.g., duplicated activities), and user-driven feedback mechanisms (e.g., rating model quality) were proposed.

Finally, improvements to **visualization and knowledge representation** were suggested. Participants noted that the knowledge graph and relationships between processes and subprocesses could be made clearer, for example through better labeling, differentiation of node types, or inclusion of version-specific information.

  

However, some concerns were raised regarding **over-reliance on automatically generated models**. Participants noted that generating process models without fully understanding the underlying documents could introduce bias or lead to superficial modelling. This highlights the importance of maintaining user awareness and control, for example by clearly indicating which parts of the input were considered or omitted.

  

Despite these concerns, the system’s **ease of use, transparency of text–model linkage, and support for iterative development** were seen as strong motivators for future adoption. In particular, features such as versioning, traceability of inputs, and structured organization of related models were perceived as valuable for real-world applications. With additional improvements—especially in usability, transparency, and AI support—participants indicated that the system has strong potential to become a practical tool for document-based process modelling.



Beyond these functionalities, participants highlighted the system’s support for iterative model development as a particularly valuable aspect. In particular, versioning was perceived as useful for tracking changes and revisiting previous model states. In addition, the logging of prompts was found to support the iterative refinement process by allowing users to review previous inputs and better understand how models were generated.


A key strength of the system lies in its support for a structured workflow that integrates document interaction with process modeling activities. Participants were able to select relevant text segments, generate initial models, and iteratively refine them within a unified environment. This integration was perceived as helpful for organizing the modeling process and maintaining a connection between source material and model development.

  

In addition, features related to versioning were consistently highlighted as valuable. Participants appreciated the ability to create and manage multiple versions of process models, as well as to track previous prompts and changes. These functionalities were considered useful for maintaining an overview of the modeling process and supporting iterative work.

  

Furthermore, participants highlighted the integration of textual and prompt information into the model representation as a valuable aspect of the system. By embedding the modeling context within the model itself, the system enables users to better relate generated structures to the underlying inputs.

  

Overall, while participants identified several areas for improvement, the system provides a functional foundation for supporting document-based process modeling, particularly in terms of integrating document interaction and model management within a single environment.

## X.2.2 Key Challenges in Use and Interaction

  > Despite the generally positive perception of the system, participants also reported several challenges in use and interaction. Some interaction steps were perceived as slightly unintuitive, for example when users needed to manually adjust or reselect elements before triggering new actions. While these issues were not considered critical, they introduced minor friction in the interaction process. In addition, the use of different AI-supported functionalities required some initial familiarization. Participants noted that understanding how and when to apply different interaction modes or prompts could take time, particularly for first-time users.

Despite the generally positive impressions, participants reported several challenges related to system use and interaction.

  

While the system was often described as intuitive at a basic level, difficulties emerged when users engaged with more advanced functionalities. In particular, participants experienced challenges when modifying models, managing iterations, and navigating between different versions. Several participants indicated that effective use of the system required a period of familiarization. Even users with domain expertise initially struggled to understand how different components of the system interact, especially the workflow connecting text selection, model generation, and subsequent refinement.

  

In addition, certain interface elements were perceived as unintuitive. Reported issues included difficulties with selecting and deselecting text, switching between models, and interpreting visual components such as the knowledge graph. Similarly, interactions related to version handling and model selection were not always immediately clear and required additional effort to understand.

  

Beyond these interaction-related issues, participants also encountered challenges in understanding how the system behaves during use. In particular, it was often unclear how user inputs—such as selected text segments and prompts—were processed and how they influenced the generated models. Participants were uncertain which parts of the provided context were taken into account and how different operations, such as refinement and regeneration, differed in their effects.

  

This lack of transparency also extended to the system’s overall behavior during modeling tasks. Participants found it difficult to understand how specific modeling decisions were made and why certain structures were generated or modified. Furthermore, the interaction design provided limited opportunities to clarify these uncertainties during use, as the system primarily relies on single-step input–output interactions. As a result, users were not able to verify assumptions about system behavior or explore alternative interpretations.

  

Overall, these findings suggest that while the system can be used successfully after some initial familiarization, users encounter challenges not only in interacting with the interface but also in developing a clear understanding of how the system responds to their inputs during the modeling process.

## X.2.4 Suggested Improvements and Desired Features

  

Participants suggested several improvements and additional features that could enhance the system’s usability and overall effectiveness. These suggestions can be grouped into several key areas.

First, while the versioning functionality was generally perceived as useful, participants expressed the need for better support in comparing different model versions. In particular, the ability to identify and understand differences between model iterations was highlighted as a valuable addition.

  

Second, the quality of document rendering and presentation was identified as an important aspect requiring improvement. Since users rely on the system not only for model generation but also for reading and interpreting source documents, a more advanced and user-friendly document display was considered important for supporting the overall workflow.

  

Third, participants suggested enhancing document search and context selection capabilities. More advanced mechanisms for identifying relevant parts of documents—potentially through retrieval-based approaches—were seen as beneficial for improving the grounding of model generation.

  

Fourth, participants indicated that a deeper integration of AI functionalities into document interaction could further improve the system. For example, embedding AI-assisted features directly within the document view could support users in understanding and exploring source materials more effectively. While such integration was considered promising, it was also recognized as technically challenging.

  

In addition, participants highlighted the need for better support in understanding and evaluating generated models. This includes explaining modeling decisions, identifying ambiguities, and providing feedback on model quality.

## X.2.5 Adoption Potential
  

Participants expressed a generally cautious but positive attitude toward the potential use of the system in future process modelling tasks. Several indicated that they would consider using the system, ~~particularly as a support tool for generating and exploring initial process model drafts.~~


However, this willingness to adopt the system was often conditional. Participants emphasized that improvements in usability, clarity of system behavior, and support for understanding model generation would be necessary before the system could be reliably integrated into their regular workflows. In particular, uncertainties regarding how inputs are processed and how AI contributes to the resulting models were identified as factors that may limit immediate adoption.

In addition, some participants raised concerns about relying on automatically generated models without fully understanding the underlying source material. This suggests that, while the system can support early-stage modeling activities, its use in more critical or finalized modeling tasks may require additional validation and user control.

Overall, the results indicate that the system shows potential as a supportive tool, but its adoption depends on addressing the identified challenges and increasing user confidence in its outputs.
