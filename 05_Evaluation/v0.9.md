A qualitative evaluation was conducted with four expert users with both academic and practical experience in process modelling. The evaluation consisted of task-based activities followed by a questionnaire including open-ended questions to assess the system’s usability and perceived usefulness, as well as to capture participants’ expert perspectives on potential improvements and additional features.

Each evaluation session followed this procedure. It began with a brief guided walkthrough and demonstration of the system, introducing its workflow and main features. Participants were then asked to complete two tasks: an initial exploratory task designed to enable them to gain hands-on experience with the system, followed by a more realistic process modelling task based on a real-world document. If time permitted, participants could additionally use the system with their own documents.

Following task completion, participants completed a questionnaire including a series of open-ended questions regarding their experiences with the system, covering aspects they found useful or problematic, as well as suggestions for improvements and desired features. In addition, participants were asked whether they would consider using the system in future document-based process modelling tasks and to elaborate on their reasoning. Each evaluation session lasted approximately 40–50 minutes.

Overall, participants perceived the proposed system as usable and capable of supporting document-based process modelling tasks. The core modelling workflow—selecting relevant text, generating process models, and maintaining links between text and model elements—was perceived as intuitive and easy to follow, enabling participants to move smoothly from document interpretation to process model construction.

At the same time, participants also identified several limitations that affected their overall experience. These were mainly related to interaction design, document handling, and the transparency of AI-assisted modelling. Although these issues did not prevent task completion, they introduced additional effort and uncertainty during use.


Overall, the findings suggest that the system is capable of supporting document-based process modelling within an integrated environment, while also revealing areas that require further improvement.


Besides the core functionalities of the system, several additional positive aspects were reported by participants. First, the versioning functionality was highlighted as the most valuable feature, as it effectively supports iterative process model development by enabling users to track changes and revisit previous model versions. Second, the embeddation within the model XML data of relevant information such as selected text segments and prompt history is considered a well-integrated design feature by some participants. Third, logging and statistical information elicited interest among some participants due to its potential for further analysis. Finally, the network-style visualisation of relationships across models and documents was mentioned as helpful, especially in the context of larger systems.



### Challenges in Use and Interaction

Despite the generally positive perception of the system, participants also reported several challenges in use and interaction. Two

The key challenge overall was understanding the logic behind AI-assisted model modifications. While the initial generation of models from selected text was generally well understood, the subsequent AI-assisted modifications were not immediately intuitive. In these situations, the system’s input logic lacked transparency, making it unclear to participants whether and how the current model and the selected text were included in the generation process. This made it difficult for participants to understand how specific actions influenced the generated outputs and could lead to mismatches between expected and actual output, as the system’s input composition did not always align with participants’ expectations.

In addition to these conceptual challenges, minor user interaction ()issues were identified in the interaction design. In particular, the requirement to explicitly deselect the current model before initiating a new modelling step was not intuitive and required users to understand and familiarise themselves with the system’s interaction logic. In addition, the review step following the initial generation of models was perceived by some participants as unnecessary because the generated models were already of sufficiently high quality.

### Future Use Intention

Participants indicated an intention to use the system in use cases involving the management and exploration of multiple models and their associated documents. In these cases, features such as clear text-model-linkage, process-subprocess-relationship, and model management functionalities were considered particularly useful. In contrast, for simpler use cases, such as generating a process model from a single document, the system remains a viable option but does not provide a strong advantage over existing approaches.
